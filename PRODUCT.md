# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Recruiters and hiring managers at tech companies, product studios, and design-forward organizations. They're scanning portfolios fast — often on a laptop, under time pressure, comparing multiple candidates. They need to clock discipline (UX/UI), taste, and caliber within the first scroll. They're allergic to generic; they reward distinctiveness.

## Product Purpose

Nadine's personal portfolio site. A UX/UI designer's showcase that doubles as a proof-of-craft artifact — the site itself must demonstrate the same intentionality she brings to product work. Success means a recruiter closes the tab with a clear impression: "I want to talk to her."

## Brand Personality

Sharp · Intentional · Confident

Tone: direct, no fluff, no false modesty. The design speaks before the copy does. Not cold — confident. Not loud — deliberate.

## Anti-references

- Generic Behance/Dribbble portfolio aesthetic: soft grids, pastel backgrounds, rounded cards, "I craft experiences" copy
- Corporate agency site: hero stock photo, mission statement, 6-person "our team" section
- Hipster studio minimalism: all white, 9px Helvetica, personality-free
- Heavy brutalism: ugly-on-purpose, aggressively raw — the darkness here comes from precision, not provocation

## Design Principles

1. **Design proves design.** The portfolio is itself a portfolio piece. Every layout decision, interaction, and microcopy choice is evidence of UX craft before a single case study loads.
2. **Hierarchy at 3 seconds.** Recruiters scan. Name → discipline → credibility signal must land without scrolling. Anything that slows that path gets cut.
3. **Editorial confidence, not decoration.** Bold choices made with purpose. Nothing added to look impressive; nothing removed to look minimal. Every element earns its place.
4. **Personality is a stance, not an accident.** The dark, cinematic, maximalist aesthetic is a deliberate choice that communicates taste. Never soften it into safety.
5. **Motion guides, never performs.** Animation directs attention and communicates state. No entrances for their own sake, no scroll-triggered confetti.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Particular attention to: contrast ratios on dark backgrounds (white text on near-black must clear 4.5:1), focus states visible on the dark theme, reduced-motion support via `prefers-reduced-motion` for all Framer Motion animations.
